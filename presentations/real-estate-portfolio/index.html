Returns Mechanics · Care

How a 30-year lease pays.

  • Tenant. Seitz gGmbH, a licensed German care operator running both facilities under one master agreement.
  • Source of rent. The German social-security framework (SGB II / XII). Public funds, statutorily backed, recession-insulated.
  • Tenor. 30 years on Hölderlin, lease running through 2051 on Reisensburg. Holding the asset means holding the contract.
  • Indexation. Rent re-sets every 24 months against inflation. The contract carries the inflation hedge, not the operator.
  • Vacancy risk. Zero on the asset. The operator can rotate occupants. We rent the building to the operator, full stop.

Returns Mechanics · Tourism

How a tourism apartment pays.

  • Dynamic daily pricing. Each night is priced against a live local benchmark set, refreshed continuously.
  • Min-stay logic. Sunday-split rules unlock single-night gaps without breaking weekly cadence.
  • Discount stacking. Mobile, early-bird, and weekly discounts are layered to capture the right segment without giving rate away.
  • Peak uplift. May through September carries the season. Ferien clusters and event weekends are priced as their own micro-seasons.
  • Booking velocity. A faster booking pace lifts ranking on Booking.com, which compounds into more organic bookings. The pricing engine optimises for that loop, not just for nightly rate.

Holding & Exit

Built around real rent.

  • Hold horizon. 10 years per asset, with refinance or exit decided per holding, not per portfolio.
  • Diversification. Across tenant duration (long-term care, short-stay tourism) and across tenant type (state-backed, private guest).
  • Leverage. Conservative. The contracts carry the cash flow, not the leverage stack.
